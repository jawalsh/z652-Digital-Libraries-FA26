# Week 9: Image Objects I: Raster & Vector Graphics, File Formats, Preservation and Access

## Summary

Digital libraries provide access to photographs, manuscripts, maps, posters, illustrations, and many other kinds of images. Decisions about how images are digitized, stored, and delivered affect quality, usability, file size, and long-term preservation.

This week introduces **raster and vector graphics**, pixels, resolution, bit depth, color, compression, and common image formats. We will distinguish **preservation masters** from **access derivatives** and consider what image-management choices make sense for a small CollectionBuilder project as well as a large institutional collection.

## Weekly Learning Objectives

By the end of this week, you should be able to:

- *distinguish* raster and vector graphics.
- *explain* image dimensions, resolution, bit depth, and color representation at an introductory level.
- *compare* TIFF, JPEG, PNG, and SVG and identify appropriate uses for each.
- *distinguish* lossy and lossless compression.
- *explain* the purposes of preservation masters and access derivatives.
- *identify* relevant technical and preservation metadata.
- *make* informed choices about image formats for a digital collection.

## Readings and Resources

### Required: Digital Content and Preservation

Read:

- Banerjee, Kyle, and Terry Reese Jr. *Building Digital Libraries*, 2nd ed. (2019), **Chapter 3, “Acquiring, Processing, Classifying, and Describing Digital Content”** and **Chapter 4, “Preservation Planning.”** Available through [IUCAT eBook](https://iucat.iu.edu/catalog/19362782). Read with particular attention to image-related content workflows and the distinction between creating access copies and planning for long-term stewardship.
- Library of Congress, [“3rd Edition of FADGI Still Image Digitization Guidelines Finalized”](https://blogs.loc.gov/thesignal/2023/05/fadgi-third-edition-still-image/). This short article introduces the purpose of contemporary imaging guidelines.

### Image Fundamentals (Selected Reading)

- Witten, Ian H., David Bainbridge, and David M. Nichols. *How to Build a Digital Library*, 2nd ed. (2010), **Chapter 5, §§5.1 and 5.3** (compression/transforms and images). [IUCAT access](https://kg6ek7cq2b.search.serialssolutions.com/?V=1.0&L=KG6EK7CQ2B&S=JCs&C=TC0000298940&T=marc).

Focus on the basic concepts of digital image representation and compression. You do not need to master the mathematics or algorithms. **If your reading time is limited, prioritize Banerjee and Reese and the short Library of Congress article.**

## Discussion

- Why retain a large TIFF when a JPEG looks similar in a web browser?
- When is a vector graphic preferable to a raster image?
- What technical information should be documented about an image?
- How should institutions balance image quality, storage, and access?
