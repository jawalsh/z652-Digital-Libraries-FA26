
# Week 14: CollectionBuilder: Deployment, Advanced Features, and Troubleshooting Studio

## Summary

This week, we focus on publishing, testing, troubleshooting, and extending our CollectionBuilder projects. We will examine how GitHub Pages builds and publishes CollectionBuilder sites, how to identify and resolve common technical problems, and how selected advanced features can support discovery, navigation, and interpretation.

We will also consider the difference between a site that builds successfully and a digital collection that works effectively for its users. Most class time will be devoted to collaborative project work, troubleshooting, peer testing, and consultation with the instructors.

## Weekly Learning Objectives

- _explain_ how GitHub Pages builds and publishes a CollectionBuilder site
- _diagnose_ common deployment, configuration, metadata, and file-path problems
- _use_ GitHub build logs, CollectionBuilder documentation, and browser tools to investigate errors
- _evaluate_ and _implement_ selected advanced features appropriate to a collection's content and audience
- _test_ collection navigation, discovery, object display, and contextual information
- _collaborate_ with classmates to identify problems, test solutions, and improve collection usability

## In Class

### Deployment and Troubleshooting

We will review the process by which CollectionBuilder repository files become a published website and examine techniques for identifying and resolving problems.

Topics and demonstrations will include:

- Checking GitHub Pages settings and the published site URL
- Understanding the relationship between repository changes, site builds, and published results
- Finding build status information and error messages in GitHub
- Distinguishing build failures from problems with content, metadata, or display
- Identifying common YAML, CSV, and configuration errors
- Diagnosing broken links, incorrect paths, and missing digital objects
- Checking whether changes have been published successfully

We will practice a systematic approach to troubleshooting: identify the problem, isolate its likely source, make a targeted change, and verify the result.

### Selected Advanced Features

We will explore additional CollectionBuilder features and customization options that may be useful for your projects.

Depending on student interests and project needs, demonstrations may include:

- Configuring maps and timelines for collections with appropriate metadata
- Customizing browse and discovery features
- Refining item pages and metadata displays
- Creating additional contextual or interpretive pages
- Modifying navigation and site layout
- Making targeted changes to site appearance

We will consider the metadata and configuration requirements of these features, as well as whether they improve the collection's usefulness for its intended audience.

Not every collection needs every feature. Choose enhancements that serve a clear purpose rather than adding complexity for its own sake.

### Peer Testing

Exchange your published collection with a classmate and explore it from the perspective of a new user.

Try to:

1. Identify the collection's subject, scope, and intended audience.
2. Find a particular item or type of material.
3. Understand the metadata displayed for an item.
4. Navigate between the collection's principal sections.
5. Locate contextual information about the collection.
6. Identify broken links, missing objects, confusing interfaces, or accessibility problems.

Share your observations with your partner. Focus on identifying problems that affect the collection's usefulness and suggesting practical improvements.

### Final Project Troubleshooting Studio

Most class time will be devoted to developing, testing, and refining your final project.

Prioritize:

1. Resolving deployment problems and ensuring your published site works.
2. Correcting metadata, digital object, and navigation problems.
3. Completing required contextual and interpretive content.
4. Improving usability and accessibility.
5. Implementing additional features or customizations where appropriate.

Feel free to work collaboratively during lab time. Help one another troubleshoot problems, share solutions and ideas, and ask classmates to test features or provide feedback on your collections. The instructors will also be available for questions and individual assistance.

## Project Progress

By the end of this week, aim to have:

- a successfully published CollectionBuilder site containing your intended collection objects and metadata;
- working item pages, navigation, and relevant discovery features;
- substantially complete contextual and interpretive content;
- major technical and usability problems identified and, where possible, resolved;
- a clear list of remaining tasks before final submission.

Prepare a brief demonstration of your collection for our final class meeting on December 10. You should be ready to introduce the collection, explain its purpose and intended audience, demonstrate its principal features, and discuss one significant design decision or challenge.

Continue developing and testing your project, using the final project assignment and rubric to guide your priorities.
