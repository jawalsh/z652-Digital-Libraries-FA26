
# Week 15: Final Project Studio, Presentations, and Site Polish

## Summary

Our final class meeting brings together the work of the semester as we prepare our digital library projects for submission. We will review practical quality assurance procedures, consider how to evaluate a published collection, and discuss lessons learned from developing digital libraries with CollectionBuilder.

Most of our class time will be devoted to collaborative project work, testing, troubleshooting, and consultation with the instructors. You will have an opportunity to address remaining technical problems, refine your collection's content and organization, and prepare your final submission.

Your final project consists of three components: a published CollectionBuilder site, an evaluation memo, and a recorded video presentation. All three are due at the end of the semester, according to the deadlines in Canvas. There will be no in-class presentations.

## Weekly Learning Objectives

By the end of this week, you should be able to:

- _evaluate_ a published digital collection for completeness, accuracy, usability, and accessibility
- _identify_ and _correct_ remaining technical, metadata, and content problems
- _verify_ that collection objects, navigation, discovery features, and contextual information work as intended
- _explain_ significant decisions about metadata, organization, navigation, and presentation
- _assess_ CollectionBuilder's strengths and limitations as a platform for institutional digital collections
- _prepare_ a recorded walkthrough demonstrating the collection's content, metadata, contextual pages, and advanced features

## In Class

### Final Project Review and Quality Assurance

We will briefly discuss what makes a digital collection ready for publication and use. A successful site build is not sufficient: the collection must also be accurate, understandable, navigable, and useful to its intended audience.

Review your project using the following checklist.

**Metadata and digital objects**

- Does the collection include at least 24 digitized items?
- Do the digitized objects conform to relevant digitization standards and best practices?
- Do images, documents, and other digital objects load correctly?
- Does each item include the appropriate required and optional CollectionBuilder metadata elements?
- Does the metadata conform to CollectionBuilder specifications and your project's established metadata guidelines?
- Are additional metadata fields, where used, accurately displayed?
- Do identifiers, filenames, and paths resolve correctly?

**Navigation and discovery**

- Do navigation links work?
- Can users browse and search the collection effectively?
- Do enabled maps, timelines, or other features work correctly?
- Are item pages understandable and appropriately organized?
- Can users find their way back to the collection's main pages?
- Does the collection include at least one advanced feature appropriate to its content and audience?

**Context and interpretation**

- Does the site clearly explain the collection's purpose, scope, and intended audience?
- Does it include one or more contextual pages beyond the standard CollectionBuilder pages?
- Do these pages provide useful background and interpretation?
- Are contextual pages and exhibits connected to relevant collection objects?

**Usability and publication**

- Is the site readable and navigable on different screen sizes?
- Are images accompanied by appropriate alternative text or descriptions where needed?
- Are links and interface elements understandable?
- Does the published site reflect the latest repository changes?
- Are there remaining template placeholders, broken links, or unfinished pages?

Prioritize problems that prevent users from discovering, accessing, or understanding your collection.

### Evaluation Memo

In addition to your CollectionBuilder site, the final project requires a short evaluation memo addressed to library administrators and senior IT staff.

The memo should assess CollectionBuilder as a potential platform for showcasing digital collections at the small liberal arts college described in the assignment scenario.

Drawing on your experience developing the prototype, consider:

- What are CollectionBuilder's principal strengths?
- What limitations or challenges did you encounter?
- What kinds of technical expertise, staff time, and ongoing maintenance might be required?
- How well does CollectionBuilder support the kinds of collections and services the library might wish to provide?
- Would you recommend that the library adopt CollectionBuilder? Why or why not?

Your memo must make a recommendation, supported by your experience and observations.

**Length:** No more than two single-spaced pages.

Include a link to your published CollectionBuilder site in the memo.

### Recorded Video Presentation

As part of your final project, prepare a **4–6-minute recorded video presentation** demonstrating your completed CollectionBuilder site.

Your presentation should:

1. Provide a brief overview of the **content, significance, and intended audience** of your collection.
2. Show one or more collection items and discuss their **metadata**, particularly any additional fields you have used beyond the standard CollectionBuilder fields.
3. Demonstrate the **contextual pages and exhibits** you created.
4. Demonstrate **one or more advanced CollectionBuilder features** you implemented.
5. Discuss significant **challenges, accomplishments, or discoveries** from developing your project.

**Your presentation must not exceed 6 minutes.**

A straightforward screen recording with narration is sufficient. You do not need to prepare slides or produce a professionally edited video.

Post a link to your video in the **Final Project Presentation** discussion in Canvas, following the instructions and deadline in the Final Project assignment. The video is not due during our final class meeting.
