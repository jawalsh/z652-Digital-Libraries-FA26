
# Week 15: Final Project Studio, Presentations, and Site Polish

## Summary

Our final class meeting brings together the work of the semester as we share, evaluate, and refine our digital library projects.

Each student will give a brief demonstration of their CollectionBuilder site, introducing the collection, explaining important decisions about its organization and presentation, and reflecting on the challenges of developing a digital library.

The remainder of class will be devoted to final project work. We will review practical quality assurance procedures and use the remaining time to test collections, resolve problems, and make improvements before submission. The instructors will be available for individual consultation and troubleshooting.

## Weekly Learning Objectives

- _present_ a digital collection and explain its purpose, audience, content, and organization
- _justify_ significant decisions about metadata, navigation, display, and contextualization
- _compare_ different approaches to digital collection design and implementation
- _evaluate_ a published collection for completeness, accuracy, usability, and accessibility
- _identify_ and _correct_ remaining technical or content problems
- _reflect_ on the challenges of developing and maintaining a digital collection

## In Class

### Final Project Presentations

Each student will give a brief presentation of their digital collection.

**Presentation format: 3 minutes, followed by approximately 1 minute for questions.**

Your presentation should address:

1. **Collection and audience:** What is your collection about, and who is it intended to serve?
2. **Content:** What kinds of digital objects does it contain, and how did you select them?
3. **Metadata and organization:** How have you described and organized the materials?
4. **Demonstration:** Show one or two important features of your published site.
5. **Reflection:** Explain one significant design decision, technical challenge, or lesson learned.

These are informal project demonstrations rather than formal presentations. Focus on showing and explaining your collection rather than attempting to cover every feature.

As you watch your classmates' presentations, consider the different approaches to metadata, collection organization, navigation, contextualization, and digital object presentation. What solutions or ideas might be useful for your own project?

### Final Quality Assurance

Before submitting a digital collection, it is important to verify not only that the site is published, but also that its content and features work as intended.

Review your project using the following checklist.

**Metadata and digital objects**

- Are all required objects represented in the collection?
- Do images, documents, and other digital objects load correctly?
- Are metadata values complete, consistent, and accurately displayed?
- Do identifiers, filenames, and paths resolve correctly?
- Are rights and source information appropriately documented?

**Navigation and discovery**

- Do navigation links work?
- Can users browse and search the collection effectively?
- Do enabled maps, timelines, or other features work correctly?
- Are item pages understandable and appropriately organized?
- Can users find their way back to the collection's main pages?

**Context and interpretation**

- Does the site clearly explain the collection's purpose, scope, and intended audience?
- Does it provide appropriate background and contextual information?
- Are the selection and organization of materials explained?
- Are contextual pages connected to relevant collection objects?

**Usability and publication**

- Is the site readable and navigable on different screen sizes?
- Are images accompanied by appropriate alternative text or descriptions where needed?
- Are links and interface elements understandable?
- Does the published site reflect the latest repository changes?
- Are there remaining template placeholders, broken links, or unfinished pages?

Prioritize problems that prevent users from discovering, accessing, or understanding your collection.

### Final Project Studio

Use the remaining class time to complete and refine your final project.

Possible tasks include:

- Correcting metadata and digital object problems
- Resolving broken links or deployment issues
- Improving navigation and discovery features
- Revising contextual and interpretive pages
- Addressing accessibility and usability concerns
- Testing the published site
- Reviewing final project requirements and preparing your submission

Feel free to work collaboratively during lab time. Help one another troubleshoot problems, share solutions and ideas, and ask classmates to test features or provide feedback on your collections. The instructors will also be available for questions and individual assistance.

Focus on completing the essential requirements and ensuring that your collection works reliably before undertaking optional enhancements.

## Final Project Submission

Complete and submit your final project according to the instructions and deadline in the Final Project assignment.

Before submitting:

- Review the assignment requirements and rubric.
- Verify that your published CollectionBuilder site is accessible.
- Test links, digital objects, navigation, and collection features.
- Check metadata for completeness, consistency, and accuracy.
- Make sure required contextual and documentation materials are included.
- Confirm that your final changes have been published successfully.
- Include all required submission materials and links.

Your final project should demonstrate not only the technical skills developed during the semester, but also thoughtful decisions about the selection, description, organization, presentation, and use of digital collections.
